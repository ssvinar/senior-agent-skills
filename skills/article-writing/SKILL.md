# article-writing

## Rules

- Open with the concrete thing: an example, output, number, code, screenshot, or anecdote. Explain after it.
- Proof over adjectives. Never invent facts, credibility, or customer evidence.
- Keep sentences short unless the target voice is deliberately expansive.
- Take the voice from the supplied examples or brand guidance. With none, write plainly, concretely, without sentiment.

## Cut on sight

- openers like "In today's rapidly evolving landscape"
- "game-changer", "cutting-edge", "revolutionary"
- "here's why this matters" as a bridge
- fake vulnerability arcs; closing questions added only for engagement
- biography that does not advance the argument
- throat-clearing before the point

## Process

1. Pin down audience and purpose.
2. Outline with one job per section.
3. Start each section with proof, an artifact, a conflict, or an example.
4. Add a sentence only if it earns its place.
5. Remove anything templated, overexplained, or self-congratulatory.

## By format

- Technical guide: say first what the reader gets; show code, commands, or output in each major section; end with actions, not a recap.
- Essay or opinion: start from a tension, contradiction, or specific observation; one argument per section; back opinions with evidence.
- Newsletter: the first screen must deliver; no diary opening; section labels only where they help scanning.

## Before delivering

- Every factual claim traces to a provided source.
- No generic AI transitions remain.
- The voice matches the examples or guidance.
- Each section adds something new.
- Formatting fits the medium.
